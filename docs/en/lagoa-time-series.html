7 Time series

The annual maps form a 40-year series for each class. land_time_series() extracts the series of interest, here soybean (39), pasture (15) and forest formation (3):

series <- land_time_series(areas$time_series, classes = c(39, 15, 3),
                           language = "en")
series$plot

Association between two series

Two trending series often look related even when they have nothing to do with each other from one year to the next. That is why series_correlation() can measure association on the levels, on the annual differences or on the residuals of a trend, and corrects the p-value for autocorrelation:

correlation <- series_correlation(series, 39, 15, transformation = "difference",
                                  language = "en")
correlation$plots$association

On annual differences, the correlation between soybean and pasture is r = -0.12 (p = 0.46; corrected p = 0.52). In other words, the replacement of one class by the other is clear in the levels, over several years, but their year-to-year changes do not move together.

Regimes and change points

series_fit_regime() fits a continuous curve with transitions between regimes. The probabilistic bent-cable model estimates when each transition happens and how long it lasts, with bootstrap confidence intervals:

fit <- series_fit_regime(series, 39, model = "prob_bent_logistic",
                         bootstrap = 200, seed = 1, language = "en")
fit$plot
Transition Year 95% CI lower 95% CI upper Rate before (km²/yr) Rate after (km²/yr)
1 2000.08 1999.48 2000.62 0.10 3.59
2 2003.11 2002.61 2003.96 3.59 -2.97
3 2006.35 2005.78 2007.91 -2.97 0.34

The soybean story has three transitions, all between 2000 and 2007: first a rapid expansion of 3.6 km² per year, then a contraction and, from 2006 on, slow growth. With model = "auto", the function compares the available models by AICc and picks the most suitable one.

Diagnostics and projection 

diagnostics <- series_diagnostics(fit)
diagnostics$plots$acf

The fit explains 98% of the variation (R²), but the residuals are autocorrelated (Durbin-Watson = 1.03; Ljung-Box, p < 0.001). When residuals depend on each other, intervals tend to come out too narrow. In that case, bootstrap_type = "block" resamples blocks of consecutive years and produces more realistic intervals.

Finally, series_forecast() extends the last regime:

projection <- series_forecast(fit, horizon = 5)
projection$plot

The projection assumes the current regime continues. Read it as a scenario, not as a prediction of future land use change.
