6 Composition and diversity

Ecology offers good tools for describing the composition of a landscape. class_diversity() treats classes as species and their areas as abundances and, for each year, computes richness, Shannon entropy, Pielou evenness, Simpson indices, Hill numbers and dominance:

diversity <- class_diversity(areas, language = "en")
diversity$plots$indices

The table diversity$change summarises the difference between the first and the last year:

Index 1985 2024 Change
Richness (classes) 8.00 11.00 3.00
Shannon (H’) 1.27 1.26 -0.01
Pielou (J’) 0.61 0.53 -0.08
Hill, q = 1 (effective classes) 3.55 3.52 -0.02
Berger-Parker (dominance) 0.58 0.64 0.06

At first sight, the landscape became more varied, going from 8 to 11 classes. Yet its effective diversity barely moved: Shannon entropy stayed flat and evenness fell from 0.61 to 0.53. The new classes take up little space, while most of the area is concentrated in a single dominant class, which was pasture in 1985 and soybean in 2024.

The entropy decomposition shows how much each class contributes to diversity over time:

diversity$plots$contribution

Hill numbers express diversity as effective classes: order 0 is richness, order 1 weights classes by area (exponential of Shannon) and order 2 gives even more weight to the dominant ones (inverse Simpson). The further the curves move from richness, the more a few classes dominate the landscape:

diversity$plots$hill

Composition over time, with Berger-Parker dominance on top, shows the change of dominant class:

diversity$plots$dominance
