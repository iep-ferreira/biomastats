10 Vectorization and connectivity

Fragments as polygons

To analyse connectivity, fragments need to become polygons. reclass_to_polygons() performs that conversion from the reclassified maps and keeps area, year and class identity. Here, the 2024 forest:

polygons <- reclass_to_polygons(metrics, years = 2024, save_shp = FALSE)

There are 28 fragments, totalling 107 ha. With save_shp = TRUE, each year is also written as a shapefile.

Connectivity at several thresholds

land_connectivity() links fragments that lie closer than a maximum distance, that is, the distance an organism can travel outside the forest, and computes connectivity indices for each threshold:

connectivity <- land_connectivity(polygons, max_dist = c(100, 300, 600),
                                  language = "en")
connectivity$plot
Maximum distance (m) Components Isolated fragments IIC (×10⁻³)
100 15 9 1.40
300 6 2 1.93
600 4 0 2.11

For a species that only crosses 100 m of open land, the forest splits into 15 disconnected groups, with 9 isolated fragments. At 300 m, 6 groups remain, and the integral index of connectivity (IIC) becomes 1 times larger. From 300 to 600 m, the gain is small: for species able to cross about 300 m, the remaining forest already works as a network.

Priority fragments

patch_imp_fast() measures how much connectivity would drop if each fragment were removed, in the chosen scenario:

scenario_300 <- connectivity$scenarios[["300"]]$land
importance <- patch_imp_fast(scenario_300, metric = "IIC")

Losing either of the two most important fragments would cut the IIC by 67.5% and 17.0%, respectively, while 21 of the 28 fragments reduce it by less than 5% each. The forest’s connectivity therefore depends on conserving a handful of fragments.

land_connectivity() already computes this prioritization for each threshold, and the map shows how much the index would drop without each fragment:

connectivity$importance_plot

The IIC follows the definition of Pascual-Hortal and Saura (2006): isolated fragments also count, through their own area. The lconnect value, which omits them, is kept in connectivity$metrics$IIC_lconnect.

Stepping stones and importance fractions

Each fragment’s importance splits into three parts (Saura and Rubio, 2010): the fragment’s own area (intra), its direct links to the others (flux), and its role as a stepping stone, when it lies on the best path between two others (connector). The probability of connectivity (PC; Saura and Pascual-Hortal, 2007) accounts for indirect paths and is the most suitable metric to identify stepping stones. Here, with a 5% dispersal probability at 300 m:

pc <- patch_imp_fast(scenario_300, metric = "PC", p_ref = 0.05, d_ref = 300)
head(pc$fractions)
         intra        flux   connector
1 1.8372799110 1.296289587 0.021202358
2 0.0043719360 0.099572204 0.000000000
3 0.0002732374 0.008390369 0.000000000
4 0.0174865998 0.423572717 0.002517258
5 0.5532703550 2.090682973 0.428980516
6 0.0002732213 0.085855429 0.000000000

In 0 fragments, more than half of the importance comes from the connector role: small patches that matter more for their position than for their area. In land_connectivity(), the fractions are stored in patch_metrics (columns dPC_intra, dPC_flux and dPC_connector) and in fraction_metrics, with the fractions_plot and connector_plot charts:

connectivity_pc <- land_connectivity(polygons, max_dist = 300,
  metric_patch = c("IIC", "PC"), p_ref = 0.05, d_ref = 300, language = "en")
connectivity_pc$fractions_plot

PC is the most demanding index. In landscapes with hundreds of fragments, parallel = 4 (or another number of processes) splits the work across cores and gives the same result; with a few dozen fragments, starting the processes costs more than it saves. Exact pruning of PC links (prune = "auto") is applied on its own from 50 fragments upwards.

Equivalent connected area and landscape fractions

The equivalent connected area (ECA; Saura et al., 2011) expresses IIC and PC in hectares: the area of a single fragment that would give the same connectivity. In metrics, ECA_IIC and ECA_PC are in m², and ECAnorm_IIC and ECAnorm_PC give it as a percentage of habitat. The PC_intra, PC_direct and PC_step columns split the index, as in Makurhini, into the area of the fragments themselves, direct links and the gain through stepping stones. Prot, ProtConn, ProtUnconn and RelConn follow ProtConn (Saura et al., 2017), with the forest taken as the protected area:
